# How crypto operators buy game content

Label: wayfinder:research
Type: research
Status: closed
Blocked by: none
Assignee: MrSufferer

## Question

How do crypto casinos, sportsbooks and prediction platforms buy games from outside providers, and could a solo dev sell them a 30-second Round game?

Cover:

- The usual deal: revenue share on gross gaming revenue, flat licence, setup fee, and typical percentages.
- Aggregators (such as Hub88, SoftSwiss, Slotegrator) versus direct integration, and what each requires of a new provider.
- Supplier licences and certification (Curaçao, Anjouan, Malta B2B, RNG or provably fair audits) operators expect, and their cost.
- How crash games and in-house "originals" are sourced, and whether small studios sell them.
- What a pilot looks like and who at an operator decides it (titles, channels, events, Telegram groups).
- Prediction platforms (Limitless, Myriad, Opinion, Polymarket and others): do they buy or license market formats or feeds from outside?

## Resolution

The standard deal is a revenue share on the operator's gross gaming revenue (GGR). Hub88 and Slotegrator publish no percentages; the only numbers are secondary: 10-15% of GGR for premium content, and an assumed 10% to the studio after the aggregator's 4%. Aggregators (Hub88, SoftSwiss, Slotegrator) gate on a supplier licence plus a lab certificate (eCOGRA, GLI, iTech Labs, BMM). Licence costs, all secondary: Curaçao about EUR 4.6k plus EUR 24.5k a year, Malta EUR 5k plus EUR 25-35k a year, Anjouan about EUR 17.8k a year. A $0 solo dev can't clear that route.

The one open door found is Stake's Engine platform: sign in with Google, GitHub or Discord, upload static files, pass review. The developer gets 10% of GGR or a guaranteed 7.5% of expected GGR (first-party site). Catches: it is exclusive to Stake, and whether it accepts a Round game and who owns the IP is unverified. Prediction platforms (Polymarket, Limitless, Myriad, Opinion): no evidence they buy or license market formats; they offer API or builder access only. Nothing first-party on how pilots run or who signs them at an operator, so treat that as outreach questions.

Unverified: Engine's actual terms, regulator-published fees (all fee figures secondary), Hub88's certification and go-live steps, and one page blocked with a 403 (Idea Usher).
