# Which candidates survive the kill rule

Status: resolved
Blocked by: 01, 02, 03, 07, 08, 09

## Question

Given [Whether free seeding undercuts a paid forecast](01-whether-free-seeding-undercuts-a-paid-forecast.md), [Who already runs conviction markets](02-who-already-runs-conviction-markets.md), [Which long-tail niches have a payer](03-which-long-tail-niches-have-a-payer.md), [Where futarchy and niche decision markets have a payer](07-where-futarchy-and-niche-decision-markets-have-a-payer.md), [Which traditional betting formats are missing onchain](08-which-traditional-betting-formats-are-missing-onchain.md), and [Whether AI-agent trading arenas have a payer](09-whether-ai-agent-trading-arenas-have-a-payer.md), which of candidates A, B, C, D, and E pass both parts of the kill rule? For each survivor, which payer type and roughly 5 named targets go to outreach? If C, D, or E survives, which single niche does it take?

First question: the research so far shows people pay for forecasters (Metaculus, Good Judgment, prize pools) and bettors pay for betting, but nobody pays an outside vendor for market liquidity. Should a candidate that sells to funders who already pay for forecasts, using a market as the way the forecast is produced, join the comparison?

## Answer

**One survivor: candidate F, sold to funders who already buy forecasts, in the AI capability and release-timing niche.** It goes to outreach alone.

Kill rule as applied (now also in the map's Notes):
- (i) is a hard 12-month cutoff (from 2025-10-20). Paid forecast prize pools count as "paid for something similar". Older payments make someone a target, not evidence.
- (ii) "Thin" is not "missing". If an incumbent lists the market at all, the rule applies.
- (iii) A known legal block that would stop any entrant kills the candidate.

Verdicts:
- **A. Budgeted forecast:** killed by (i) and (ii). Seer and Futarchy Labs seed liquidity free, and no outside forecast fee was paid in the window.
- **B. Conviction market:** killed by (i). Nobody paid for one, and Polymarket's free 4% holding reward touches (ii).
- **C. Long-tail niche:** folded into F. C's best niches (AI capability, research funding) are places where forecasts are bought, not markets.
- **D. Futarchy niches:** killed. MetaDAO's liquidity is bundled into the raise (ii), and every other niche fails (i).
- **E. Trader-paid:** killed. Pari-mutuel horse racing is blocked by the Interstate Horseracing Act (iii). F1 per-race already has Polymarket markets (about $234K on one race winner), so it fails (ii). AI arenas, parlays, in-play, props, casino-style binaries and esports have incumbents (ii).
- **F. Forecast buyers (new):** passes, weakly. For (i), the Metaculus AI benchmark's $58K prize pool (Spring 2026, API credits from OpenAI and Anthropic) is in the window. Coefficient Giving's FRI grants ($11M+) and its Metaculus Pro commission (Sep 2025) are just outside it. For (ii), Metaculus and Good Judgment charge for forecasts, so no incumbent gives this free. The product is a forecast on the funder's question, produced by a market rather than a forecaster panel.

Outreach targets for F (five):
1. Coefficient Giving, AI governance team: the strongest repeat buyer of forecasts.
2. Forecasting Research Institute: runs AI expert panels, so it could be a buyer or a competitor.
3. Anthropic: sponsored the Metaculus AI benchmark.
4. OpenAI: sponsored the same benchmark.
5. Metaculus: approached as a channel partner for a market-based question series, not as a buyer.

Dropped: Bridgewater (its contest was a talent search, not a purchase of information) and Good Judgment (a competitor). Research funding, F's runner-up niche, is not added because its only payer is Coefficient Giving again.

If funders say no, [One buyer and one wedge](06-one-buyer-and-one-wedge.md) still decides by 2026-11-03. "No buyer" is a valid result there.
