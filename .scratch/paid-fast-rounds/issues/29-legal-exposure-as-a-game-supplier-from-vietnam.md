# Legal exposure as a game supplier from Vietnam

Label: wayfinder:research
Type: research
Status: closed
Blocked by: none
Assignee: MrSufferer

## Question

Does selling game software to a licensed or offshore operator, without taking any bettor money, change the legal risk for a developer living in Vietnam?

Start from [Vietnam law for points and real-money group markets](../../group-markets/issues/02-vietnam-law-for-points-and-real-money-group-markets.md) and [Legal path for real-money creator markets](../../creator-feed-markets/issues/20-legal-path-for-real-money-creator-markets.md). Cover: whether supplying gambling software counts as organising or abetting gambling under Vietnamese law; known cases of Vietnamese developers working for offshore gaming firms; whether being paid a revenue share differs from a flat fee; what operators' supplier licences would ask of a Vietnamese resident; and the cheapest way to get a lawyer's view. Payers that aren't operators (sponsors, streamers, crypto projects) count too, if their risk differs.

## Resolution

Not taking bettor money helps but does not clear a developer living in Vietnam. Penal Code Art. 17 makes anyone who "creates material conditions" an accomplice, and Art. 322(1)(d) lists "using means to assist gambling". Liability turns on intent and knowledge. The agent read the official consolidated Code (VBHN 135/2025) for Arts. 6, 17, 285, 288, 321 and 322; Art. 6 says "may", not "shall", for crimes abroad by citizens.

Cases found are all operations, not lone freelancers: Da Nang, Nov 2025 (about 63 staff building betting sites for a Cambodian buyer, charged under Art. 288) and Hung Yen, indictment 21 Oct 2026 (74 defendants, including devs who allegedly knew about gambling ad income, charged with organising gambling). No lone-contractor case found. A revenue share looks worse than a flat fee, but neither is a defence: Art. 322 jumps to 5-10 years at VND 50m of profit or when the internet is used. Non-operator payers (sponsor prizes with no player stake) carry lower risk; any money-staked game carries the same risk.

Supplier licences (Curaçao, Malta) mostly vet owners, key persons and equity holders, with criminal-record and source-of-funds checks (consultant summaries only). Curaçao foreign suppliers must register by 24 Dec 2026. A first criminal-law consult in Vietnam costs roughly US$20-200 (law-firm price lists). The note ends with 7 questions for the lawyer.

Unverified: Decree 282/2025 text, regulator pages (CGA, MGA), court-judgment databases, the charge articles in the two cases, Art. 324 money laundering, tax treatment, and 2026 draft amendments. Not legal advice.
